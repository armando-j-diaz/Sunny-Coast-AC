Miami AC Report: what breaks most in South Florida ACs

By Sunny Coast AC team · November 25, 2026 · Data: January 2025 to November 5, 2026

We looked at 3,009 finished AC repair jobs our team did, almost all in Miami-Dade and Broward. For each one, we sorted the tech’s notes into what was actually wrong. Here is what we found.

Key findings

  • Clogged drain lines were the #1 problem: 22% of jobs, about 1 in 5. Counting drain pans, float switches, pumps and water damage too, about 1 in 4 jobs (26.8%) had a water problem.
  • About 1 in 11 visits found nothing broken (8.8%). Easy fixes like a dirty filter, a tripped breaker or a thermostat setting came up too.
  • Living near the beach didn’t mean more refrigerant leaks. Leaks showed up on 10.5% of inland jobs and 7.8% of coastal ones.
  • Electrical problems nearly doubled in winter: 13.1% of December–March jobs vs 6.9% in June–September.
  • About 76% of calls came from May to October (January–October, both years).

What our team found most

Share of 3,009 AC jobs where the tech found or fixed each problem. One job can have more than one problem, so the numbers add up to more than 100%.

Why drain lines? An AC pulls water out of the air, and Miami air holds a lot of it. That water runs out through a thin pipe that stays warm and wet, so slime grows inside. Read more in AC leaking water inside? It’s usually a clogged drain line.

Coast vs inland

We expected salt air to cause more refrigerant leaks near the water. It didn’t. Inland homes had a bit more leaks. Coastal homes had a bit more dirty outdoor coils, water damage and mold. The gap is small: humidity hits every home here.

Summer vs winter

In summer, ACs run nonstop, so we saw more water damage, mold and dirty outdoor coils. In winter, electrical problems jumped: 13.1% of jobs vs 6.9% in summer. Thermostat problems more than doubled too (7.1% vs 3.1%). One likely reason: many people switch to heat for the first time in months, and weak parts show up then.

What this means for your home

  • Keep the drain line clear. It is the #1 problem by far. A yearly tune-up flushes it.
  • Check the easy things first. About 1 in 11 visits found nothing broken. Here are 5 things to check before you call.
  • Test the heat before a cold snap. Winter brings more electrical and thermostat problems.
  • If water is leaking, turn the AC off at the thermostat and call before it reaches the ceiling or floor.

How we counted

  • Jobs: 3,027 finished AC jobs our team did, almost all in Miami-Dade and Broward, January 2025 to November 5, 2026. 3,009 had tech notes, and those are the base for every percentage.
  • Sorting: an AI tool sorted each tech’s notes into a list of problems found or fixed. Small numbers are rough.
  • One job, many problems: a job can count in more than one row.
  • Brands: counted from job photos where the brand was clear, once per job.
  • Privacy: no names, addresses or customer details. Only totals are shared.
