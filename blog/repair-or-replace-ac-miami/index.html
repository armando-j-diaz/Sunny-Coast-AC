Repair or Replace Your AC? How to Decide in Miami

By Sunny Coast AC team · November 19, 2026

Your AC broke again, and now you have a choice: pay for the repair, or put that money toward a new system. Here is how to decide, from a team that does both every day in Miami-Dade and Broward.

Below: a quick answer, the rules of thumb people use (and where they fall short), which repairs are worth it, what a new system can save, the Miami things that matter, and what to do next.

Safety first. If your AC is tripping the breaker, smells like burning, or makes a loud buzz or bang, turn it off at the thermostat and call a pro. Don't open the unit or touch the wiring. Deciding to repair or replace can wait until it is safe.

The short answer

Repair it if the system is under about 10 years old, the fix is small, and it has been reliable. Think about replacing it if it is over 10 to 15 years old, needs a big repair like a compressor or coil, or keeps breaking.

That 10-year mark is not just our opinion. ENERGY STAR says to consider a new system when yours is more than 10 years old, needs frequent repairs, or your bills keep going up. The EPA says a system over 10 years old, or one that isn't keeping you comfortable, should be checked by a pro.

Most of the time, the answer is a simple AC repair. In the last 12 months, our team answered 1,344 first-time AC repair calls (not counting return visits or second opinions) and installed 126 new systems. That's about 1 new system for every 11 calls, or about 9%. The most common fixes were simple ones, like clearing a clogged drain line or replacing a bad capacitor.

Why repairs stop making sense

An AC wears out a little every hour it runs. Motors, coils, and the compressor all have a limited life. Early on, a repair fixes the one part that broke, and the rest of the system still has years left.

Later, the picture changes. When one big part fails on an old system, the other old parts are close behind. You can end up paying for one repair this year and another next year, while the old system keeps using more power than a new one would.

How long does an AC last in Miami?

Expect about 10 to 15 years here, which is shorter than in cooler places. An HVAC industry site, HVAC.com, puts South Florida systems at about 10 to 15 years, compared with 15 to 20 in milder parts of the country.

The reasons are simple:

  • It runs almost all year. Up north, an AC might rest for half the year. In Miami, it barely gets a break.
  • Humidity. The AC pulls water out of the air all day. That keeps the drain line and coil wet, which helps slime and rust grow.
  • Salt air. Homes near the water, like in Miami Beach, Key Biscayne, or Hollywood, see salt eat at the outdoor coil and cabinet.

The 50% rule (and the $5,000 rule)

The 50% rule is the one we like best: if the repair costs more than half the price of a new system, replacing is usually the better buy. CNN Underscored quotes an expert who has seen this many times.

You may also hear about the $5,000 rule: multiply the age of your system by the repair cost. If the answer is more than 5,000, lean toward replacing. The same CNN guide shares it.

We think $5,000 leans too fast toward a new system, so we would push that line up to about $7,000. Here is the rough math. On a 15-year-old system, 7,000 divided by 15 is about 467. So on a system that old, a repair over about $470 would point toward replacing. On a 5-year-old system, the line is about $1,400.

These rules are a starting point, not the final word. They don't know if your system has been reliable, if a warranty covers the part, or how long you plan to stay in the home. Use them to frame the choice, then look at the whole picture.

Repairs that are usually worth it

Small, common fixes are almost always worth doing, even on an older system. These are some of the most common problems our team fixed from January 2025 to early November 2026, based on our job records:

  • Clogged drain line (the #1 problem we found, 22% of jobs)
  • Bad run capacitor (7.4%)
  • Dirty indoor coil (6.7%), which a yearly tune-up helps prevent
  • Clogged drain pan (5.4%)
  • Dead thermostat (2.8%). Try the 5 safe checks before you call first.

None of these mean the system is done. Fix it and keep going. And on 8.8% of jobs, our techs found no mechanical problem at all.

The big repairs: compressor and coil

A failed compressor or a leaking coil is where the real decision comes in. The compressor is the heart of the outdoor unit. The coils are where the cooling happens. These are the biggest repairs an AC can need.

On a newer system, a big repair often still makes sense, more so if the part is under the maker's warranty. Most new systems come with a parts warranty of 5 to 10 years, says CNN Underscored. On a system over 10 to 15 years old, the same repair is often money better spent on a new system.

Signs it is time to replace

One sign alone doesn't decide it. Two or three together usually do.

1. It is over 10 to 15 years old. Look at the label on the outdoor unit. The date is often in the serial number, and we can read it for you.

2. It needs a big repair. Compressor, outdoor coil, or indoor coil.

3. It keeps breaking. A repair every summer adds up fast.

4. Your bills keep climbing. ENERGY STAR lists rising bills with frequent repairs as a sign.

5. Some rooms are too hot, or the house feels sticky. ENERGY STAR lists uneven rooms and humidity problems as signs too.

6. It uses R-22. More on that below.

When to fix it

Fix it when the system is under 10 years old, the repair is small, and it has been reliable. Also fix it when the part is under warranty, or when you plan to sell the home soon and just need it running well.

Replacing a system that only needs a small repair is not a good use of your money. A good tech should tell you that.

What if my AC uses R-22?

You don't have to replace it just because it uses R-22, but repairs that need refrigerant get harder. R-22 (often called Freon) has not been made or imported in the U.S. since January 1, 2020, says the EPA. Recycled and older supply is still used for repairs, and the EPA says it does not require homeowners to replace working equipment.

R-22 systems are still common here. We estimate about 1 in 4 homes in South Florida still runs on one. If one of these needs a big refrigerant repair, we usually talk about replacing it, because it is likely 15 or more years old.

Newer systems are changing too. Starting January 1, 2025, new home AC systems must use refrigerants that are better for the climate, per EPA rules. That rule is for new equipment. If your current system works well, you don't need to replace it because of it.

Will a new AC lower my power bill?

Yes, usually, and more the older your system is. ENERGY STAR says high-efficiency units, installed correctly, can save up to 20 percent on heating and cooling costs (ENERGY STAR). The EPA says an ENERGY STAR system can cut a yearly energy bill by more than $160, depending on where you live (EPA).

In Miami, where the AC runs most of the year, savings matter more. We walk through the rough savings math for an old vs new system in our FPL rebate post.

One big catch: the install matters as much as the rating. A NIST study found install mistakes can raise heating and cooling energy use by about 30 percent. It also found leaky ducts raise indoor humidity in hot, humid places like ours.

How to spot a bad call

Bad advice can go both ways. Watch for these:

  • Pushed to replace a young system. If your AC is a few years old and someone says it must be replaced, ask why in writing, and get a second look.
  • Refrigerant "top-offs" every year. An AC doesn't use up refrigerant. If it needs more, there is a leak. The EPA says major leaks rarely happen in well-installed, well-kept units. Ask to find and fix the leak.
  • A new system with no permit. A new AC system needs a permit. We pull one on every install.
  • A size picked by guess. Too big or too small both cause problems, like a sticky house. Ours are sized for your home, not guessed.
  • No price in writing. You should see the price before any work starts.

Miami-Dade and Broward extras

Help your AC last longer

A few safe habits can add years and cut down on repairs:

  • Change the air filter every 1 to 3 months. A dirty filter makes the whole system work harder.
  • Flush the drain line with vinegar about once a month. Turn the AC off at the thermostat first. Pour 1 cup of white vinegar into the drain line's access port, the short capped pipe near the indoor unit. Only do this when the drain is working, and never mix it with bleach or other cleaners.
  • Keep the outdoor unit clear. Trim plants back so air can flow. Look, but don't touch anything inside.
  • Get a yearly check. Small problems found early stay small.

Questions people ask about repair or replace

Can I replace just the outdoor unit? Sometimes, but it is often a bad idea on an older system. The indoor and outdoor parts are rated as a matched set, and a mismatched pair may not cool as well or last as long. FPL's rebate also needs a full new system.

Should I wait until my AC dies completely? If it is old and failing, planning ahead is easier. A breakdown in August means a hot house while you wait, and less time to compare options.

How long does a new system install take? Most new system installs are done in one day.

What warranty comes with each? Our repairs come with a 1-Year Repair Warranty. New systems get a 1-Year Labor Warranty plus our System Workmanship Warranty, on top of the maker's parts warranty. See our full warranty terms.

Is it safe to keep running an AC that trips the breaker or makes loud noises? No. Turn it off at the thermostat and call a pro. Running it can turn a small repair into a big one.
